Snatch It — locked direction prototype

Explore it

  • Home has three views — Editorial Market is the default; the pill row at the top switches to Liquid and Ticket Stub. They are three ways to look at the same feed, not three themes.
  • Tap the hero or any row to open Listing detail, then the glass Place bid CTA.
  • Run a transfer: Send → Mark as sent, Receive → Confirm receipt, Checkout → Pay. Each CTA goes idle → working → done.
  • Filters opens from every Home view. Reduce Motion is on by default; the toggle previews motion.

Screen map — the locked direction for each surface

Every screen below is fixed. Only Home has alternates, and they are view modes inside Home rather than app-wide themes. "Liquid" means a rounded translucent CTA anchored at the bottom plus restrained overlays and image treatment — never a layout replacement.

Shared system — the recommendation

Nine screens now carry six different layout directions. These five rules are what keep them one product rather than six.

Spacing

One 4 pt base, six steps: 4 / 6 / 9 / 12 / 16 / 22. A direction picks its rhythm from those steps and never invents a value between them.

Per direction

  • Gallery 18 / 26 — the most air, because artwork carries the screen
  • Editorial 16 / 22
  • Ledger 14 / 19
  • Liquid 14 / 20 with 14 px grouped corners
  • Utility & Index 12–13 / 16–17 — the tightest, because a money screen has to fit above its action

Row padding moves with it: 11 px Gallery, 9 px Ledger and Liquid, 7 px Utility and Index.

Typography

One scale everywhere, no exceptions: hero 21–22, screen title 16, item name 14.5, body 13, small 11.5, micro 10, price 16, dominant price 22. Regular text size only in this prototype.

Two faces, by role

A condensed display face for names on Editorial and Index only — the two directions whose identity is typographic. Everywhere else names use the system sans. Every figure is tabular so columns align.

Micro labels are the only uppercase in the product, at 10 px with 0.09 em tracking. No oversized all-caps.

CTA treatment

Glass CTA on four screens only: Listing, Send, Receive, Checkout. 20 px radius, 52 px tall, translucent with a 22 px blur, one inner top highlight, and a red glow at 60% opacity below it.

Everywhere else the action is flat — 2 px radius, the same red. Bids, Tickets, Profile, Settings and Home have no bottom action at all, so they keep the dock and nothing else.

The readability rule

Content scrolls under the glass with matched bottom padding and a 34 px scrim above the bar, so the last line of a price breakdown is never trapped behind it.

Chrome accents

Five places, and no more: the wordmark, the 1 px top edge on nav bar / action bar / dock, the selected-tab indicator, the inner highlight on a CTA, and the ring on the profile avatar.

Status colour

Chrome for live and leading, amber for attention, green only for a confirmed payment state. Red is reserved for the primary action and destructive states, so discovery contains none of it — including the ending-soon pill, which is amber.

Motion

Reduce Motion is the default and the shipping baseline. With motion on: screen change rises 4 px over 200 ms; sheets translate 14 px over 260 ms; a Home view switch crossfades at 200 ms; a changed bid value dips once.

Never

No animation on a warning, none on a per-second tick, and no motion that delays an action's result. Every transition collapses through one reduced-motion check.

What must stay on screen

Independent of direction: the delivery blocker, the expiry consequence, the proof requirement, the transfer status, the payment state and the full price breakdown — with the total set larger than its line items.

No direction in this prototype is allowed to remove one of those to look cleaner, and the four transaction screens are checked for all six.

Unresolved visual decisions — before implementation

Eight things this prototype had to decide in order to exist. Each is a real choice, and each is reversible in one place.

DecisionWhat the prototype doesWhy it needs a ruling
1 · Red for destructiveDestructive actions are outlined, never filled, and red is absent from discovery entirely — the ending-soon pill is amber.It reverses the direction approved on 14 Sep. D is carrying it as an A-or-B.
2 · Corner radiusContent stays at 0. Liquid introduces 14 px grouped lists on Profile and Settings, 16 px on Liquid-mode Home media, and 20 px on the glass CTA."Radius 0 is identity" is a stated rule. Liquid cannot exist without curvature, so the exception has to be granted deliberately.
3 · GreenGreen appears only on a confirmed payment state (verified proof, released payment). Status and live indicators are chrome or amber.You asked for chrome/amber on status. Payment confirmation is arguably status too — if so, green leaves the product.
4 · Condensed faceUsed for names on Editorial and Index only. The prototype falls back to a system condensed stack; the app's Oswald is not loaded here.Two faces by role is a system commitment, and the real face will set differently from the fallback.
5 · Blur without a buildEvery glass surface assumes a real backdrop blur.Neither expo-blur nor expo-glass-effect is installed. Without an authorised build the CTA ships as a solid translucent fill — close, but not glass.
6 · Gallery on TicketsOne 4:5 flyer per ticket, so a second ticket sits below the fold. It is the most beautiful Tickets screen and the least scannable. A wallet with eight tickets may want Ledger.
7 · Home mode persistenceThe view mode is session state and resets to Editorial Market. Whether a chosen view is remembered per person, and whether it syncs across devices, is a product decision.
8 · Where the direction label livesOutside the phone, on the plate under the device. Deliberate: nothing about the review tooling is allowed inside a frame that is meant to read as the app.
