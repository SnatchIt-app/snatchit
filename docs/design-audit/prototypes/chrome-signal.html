Snatch It — Chrome Signal interactive prototype

Try it

  • Tap the hero or any event row to open a listing, then Place bid — the sheet runs a real confirm → working → success sequence.
  • Filters on Home and a bid status row on Bids open the two glass overlays.
  • Run a transfer end to end: Send → Mark as sent, then Receive → Confirm receipt, then Checkout → Pay.
  • Switch Motion to Full to preview the transitions. Reduce Motion is the default, exactly as it would ship.

Design rationale

Every surface, accent and motion decision below exists for a stated reason. Where a choice reverses something from an earlier exploration, the reversal is named rather than quietly applied.

Foundation

Deep black #08090A for the canvas and charcoal #0F1114 / #15181C for raised surfaces. Panels are lifted with a 4% white wash rather than a border, so a screen reads as layers of light instead of a stack of boxes. Radius stays at 0 for content and pill for chips — the approved identity, untouched.

Why not more borders

A border around every container is what made the earlier work read flat: everything sat on the same plane with outlines doing the separating. Here separation comes from a hairline, a wash, or nothing at all.

Chrome, used selectively

The metallic language appears in exactly four places: the wordmark, the 1 px top edge on the navigation bar, the sticky action bar and the dock, the selected-tab indicator, and the inner highlight on the primary button. That is the whole budget.

Why so little

Chrome reads as precision only while it is rare. On every element it becomes texture, and the artwork — which is the actual product — has to fight it.

Glass, where it earns depth

Blur is on navigation and overlay surfaces only: the top bar, the dock, the sticky action bar, the floating back and photo controls, and the two sheets. Nothing in the content layer is blurred — the label over the hero is a plain translucent plate, not glass.

Why

Apple's own guidance keeps Liquid Glass in the navigation layer and out of the content layer. Chrome over artwork is also a contrast lottery; a scrim is a promise you can keep, and the app already renders one.

Red — and what changed

Red appears in two roles only: the single filled primary action on a screen, and destructive states, which are outlined rather than filled. Text on red is pure black at 5.4:1 — measured, because the near-black I started with came out at 4.08:1 and failed AA at this weight. The dock's bid count used to be red and is now chrome: it is neither an action nor a danger, so it had no claim on the colour.

The reversal

Earlier explorations let the under-fifteen-minute auction pill be red. It is gone. Urgency now escalates white → amber, so nothing in discovery can be mistaken for something you can press. Red means you are about to do something, everywhere in the product.

Live bid, truthfully

Four states, all derived from the auction's real end time: a quiet timestamp beyond six hours; a soft white dot and the remaining time inside six hours; an amber Ending in 11m pill under fifteen minutes; and a grey Ended line after it, which is terminal.

The rule

Escalation is a function of remaining time only. No "hot", no "almost gone", no countdown on an auction that is not open, and every treatment disappears at expiry rather than lingering.

Motion

Reduce Motion is the prototype's default, because it is the setting that must look finished. With motion on: screens rise 4 px over 200 ms; sheets translate 14 px over 260 ms; a replaced image crossfades; a changed bid value dips and returns once.

Two prohibitions

Nothing animates on a warning, and nothing animates on a per-second tick. A countdown that pulses every second is decoration pretending to be information.

Composition and rhythm

A 4 pt base with six steps (4/6/9/12/16/22). Discovery breathes at 16–22; transaction screens compress to 9–12 so the whole commitment fits above the action. Label left, value right, every figure tabular, so numbers form a column your eye can run down.

Image treatment

Artwork is cropped, never letterboxed: 4:5 in the hero, 4:5 thumbnails in the feed, 16:9 on a pass. One scrim ramp, one diagonal sheen at 10%, and a grain pass — enough to look printed rather than rendered.

Transaction screens

Recipient, delivery method, proof, transfer status, payment state and the full price breakdown stay visible on every transaction screen in every variant. The total is set larger than its line items — the FTC's junk-fee rule requires the total to be the most prominent price, and that is checked by measuring, not by looking.

One action

Exactly one filled action per screen, with its consequence on its own sub-line: this releases payment to the seller. A disabled action states why it is disabled in the same place.

One family, two products

The venue console uses the same canvas, the same hairlines, the same tabular figures and the same action ranks at a denser rhythm. It inverts one rule: on the consumer side red is the thing you want to press; on the console the destructive action is the rare one, so red is reserved for it and routine actions are chrome.

Honest metrics

A point-in-time count is labelled as one and carries no comparison period, because the data layer cannot produce one. Only period aggregates say "last 7 days".

The same two screens, three directions

Home and Send Transfer across Chrome Editorial, Chrome Utility and the Chrome Signal hybrid. Same content, same data, same components — only the rhythm and the surface treatment change.

Send Transfer

Recommendation — Chrome Signal

Editorial for discovery, Utility for money, Liquid Chrome for navigation and overlays only.

Why the hybrid, not either pole

Chrome Editorial alone is the most beautiful Home and the worst Send Transfer: at editorial spacing the commitment no longer fits above the action, and a money screen that scrolls before you can read the total is a worse screen, however handsome.

Chrome Utility alone is the best Send Transfer and a marketplace nobody browses: without artwork an event is a string, and the reason to want a ticket disappears.

Chrome Signal keeps the flyer doing the selling and the ledger doing the reassuring, with one type scale, one set of notice ranks and one action system across both.

Still the owner's call

  • Red for destructive. This prototype outlines destructive actions and never fills them, and it removes red from discovery entirely. That reverses the direction approved on the 14th.
  • Which density wins on Tickets. Drawn here as Editorial; Utility is defensible.
  • Whether the console follows. The dashboard view here is a proposal, not D's design.
