# SNATCH IT — Visual Direction Guide
### Making investors *feel* demand before they read the business model

> Scope: imagery and visual storytelling only. No redesign, no copy changes, no layout changes.
> This guide tells you **what to add, where, and in what style** — and just as importantly, **what to keep clean.**

---

## PART 1 — Why the reference decks feel premium

Three decks, three different routes to "premium." Read for *feeling*, not business model.

### A. Monroe x Places — *aspiration through photography*
- **Emotion:** desire, belonging, "I want to be there." Warmth, leisure, sun.
- **Atmosphere:** golden-hour Miami luxury — pools, yachts, Art-Deco hotels, editorial bodies-by-the-water shot tastefully.
- **Visual storytelling:** every slide is a *place you could be standing in.* Lifestyle first, facts second.
- **Image selection / photography:** full-bleed, one consistent **warm sepia/terracotta duotone grade.** That single grade is 80% of why it feels expensive — disparate photos become one world.
- **Pacing:** lush image → quiet text → lush image. It breathes.
- **Negative space:** generous; type floats over imagery with room around it.
- **Perceived brand value:** very high — looks like a hotel brand book, not a startup deck.
- **Investor psychology:** "this is already a world with taste and gravity; I want in." Two registers — warm editorial (aspiration) and **black-and-gold** (money/exclusivity, the membership tiers).

### B. Places Founders Club Brief — *exclusivity through restraint*
- **Emotion:** quiet power, scarcity, "you may not be allowed in."
- **Atmosphere:** austere, editorial, almost no photography.
- **Visual storytelling:** told in **confident one-line declaratives** on near-empty slides — *"Access cannot be purchased. It is granted." / "Money can't buy access." / "The ultimate luxury is time."*
- **Image selection:** deliberately minimal. The luxury IS the emptiness.
- **Pacing:** black slide → white slide → single hero stat ($2.7T / 38M / One). Rhythm of a fashion lookbook.
- **Negative space:** enormous — the loudest tool in the deck.
- **Perceived brand value:** extremely high through discipline.
- **Investor psychology:** scarcity + status. Restraint signals "we don't need to convince you."

### C. Storylines — *the cautionary "informational" register*
- **Emotion:** competence, but low desire. You're being *briefed,* not seduced.
- **Atmosphere:** navy corporate; dense charts, many stats per slide.
- **Visual storytelling:** hero renders of the ship do real work, but text density buries the feeling.
- **Image selection:** aspirational renders fight with spreadsheets on the same slide.
- **Pacing:** uniform and busy — little contrast between "feel" slides and "facts" slides.
- **Negative space:** scarce.
- **Perceived brand value:** solid/credible, not magnetic.
- **Investor psychology:** "I understand it" rather than "I want it." **This is the trap SNATCH IT is currently closest to.**

**The transferable lesson:** premium = **one consistent grade + large imagery on the emotional slides + ruthless negative space + a clear rhythm between "feel" slides and "fact" slides.** Monroe does it with photos; PLACES does it with emptiness. Either works. Density kills it.

## PART 2 — Why SNATCH IT currently feels informational, not emotional

SNATCH IT already has a *strong* art-directed system: brutalist black canvas, Helvetica-black caps, a single hot red (#ff0033), confident short headlines. That's a real asset — do not dilute it.

But right now:
1. **Only 3 of 17 slides carry imagery** (solution screenshot, product screenshots, founder photo). The other 14 are **type-on-black.** Type-on-black is precise and serious — but with no atmosphere it reads as a *document,* not a *night.*
2. **There is no felt "demand."** The deck *says* demand exists ("lives in DMs," "liquidity is the moat") but never lets the investor *feel* the crowd, the urgency, the FOMO that is literally the product.
3. **No sense of place.** This is a Miami nightlife company with zero Miami, zero nightlife, zero people on screen until the founder.
4. **All slides have the same visual weight** — like Storylines, "feel" and "fact" slides look identical, so nothing builds emotionally.

The fix is not more design. It's **a thin, disciplined layer of cinematic atmosphere on the right slides only,** in one grade, so the existing system stays intact and gains a pulse.

## PART 4 — Per-slide image direction

For each "feel" slide: **image type · photography style · mood · sourcing keywords.**

### Slide 1 — Cover (HERO)
- **Image type:** full-bleed atmospheric photograph, heavily darkened, logo + red floating over it.
- **Photography style:** cinematic, low-key, shot-on-film grain; a crowd in silhouette under a single light, or a back-of-head crowd facing a glowing stage. Faces obscured — suggestion, not documentation.
- **Mood:** anticipation the second before the drop. Electric, restrained.
- **Keywords:** `crowd silhouette stage light`, `concert backlit haze`, `nightlife cinematic moody`, `hands in the air silhouette`, `dark club single spotlight`.

### Slide 2 — One-liner
- **Image type:** quiet full-bleed or large band; minimal subject.
- **Photography style:** a single figure or a glowing skyline through motion blur; lots of black.
- **Mood:** calm confidence before the energy.
- **Keywords:** `lone figure neon night`, `city night bokeh`, `motion blur street night`, `minimal nightlife editorial`.

### Slide 3 — Problem ("lives in DMs")
- **Image type:** intimate, close, low-light — a phone screen glow on a face/hand in a dark venue.
- **Photography style:** shallow depth of field, warm phone glow, the *only* light source. Editorial, not a screenshot of a chat.
- **Mood:** the private hustle — secrecy, friction, FOMO happening off-platform.
- **Keywords:** `phone glow face dark`, `texting in club low light`, `hand phone nightlife bokeh`, `screen light on face cinematic`.

### Slide 6 — Why Now
- **Image type:** large background, energetic but controlled.
- **Photography style:** golden/blue-hour rooftop or queue forming; momentum and movement.
- **Mood:** cultural inflection — "everyone is already doing this."
- **Keywords:** `rooftop party golden hour`, `line outside venue night`, `young crowd phones up`, `festival crowd dusk`.

### Slide 7 — Market
- **Image type:** **subtle** oversized faint photo behind the stats (15–25% opacity) or a thin top/side image band — data must stay 100% legible.
- **Photography style:** wide aerial of a packed venue / festival field; scale.
- **Mood:** vastness, opportunity, "this is big."
- **Keywords:** `festival crowd aerial`, `stadium night crowd wide`, `packed venue overhead`.

### Slide 11 — GTM ("Win Miami")
- **Image type:** sense-of-place hero — large image band.
- **Photography style:** unmistakably Miami — palm + Art-Deco neon, South Beach blue hour, skyline from the water. Warm, glamorous, cinematic.
- **Mood:** home turf, glamour, beachhead.
- **Keywords:** `Miami south beach night neon`, `Miami skyline dusk water`, `art deco ocean drive night`, `Miami rooftop palms sunset`.

### Slide 16 — Vision ("Access becomes a marketplace")
- **Image type:** expansive, forward-looking full-bleed.
- **Photography style:** open horizon at blue hour, a threshold/open door of light, or a wide crowd dissolving into city lights. Aspirational, calm, big.
- **Mood:** scale + possibility; "this becomes infrastructure."
- **Keywords:** `open door light silhouette`, `city horizon blue hour`, `crowd dispersing lights`, `threshold glow cinematic`.

### Slide 17 — Ask ("$300K Pre-Seed")
- **Image type:** confident closing frame — darkened atmospheric image with the number owning the space, or near-black with one detail.
- **Photography style:** a single elegant nightlife detail — wristband, a hand on a velvet rope, a closing door with light behind it.
- **Mood:** invitation, momentum, "get in now."
- **Keywords:** `velvet rope hand night`, `wristband macro low light`, `door closing light behind`, `entrance glow minimal`.

### Subtle / optional (10, 13) and clean (8, 9, 12, 15)
- 10 Supply / 13 Moat: at most a faint abstract motion-blur or light-streak texture at low opacity. Never a literal photo competing with the diagram.
- 8, 9, 12, 15: **no imagery.** Black space + red accent + type. This contrast is what makes the "feel" slides hit.

## PART 5 — What imagery to ADD vs NOT add

### ADD (the SNATCH IT world, in one grade)
- Crowds in **silhouette / from behind**, facing light — implies demand without documenting a mess.
- **Phone glow in the dark** — the product's native habitat.
- **Golden-hour & blue-hour Miami** — rooftops, skyline, Art-Deco neon, ocean.
- **Thresholds of access** — lines, velvet ropes, wristbands, a door with light behind it (echoes the PLACES "access is granted" psychology).
- **Motion / energy** — controlled motion blur, light streaks, hands up in silhouette.
- **Macro details** — wristband, ticket, a single hand — quiet, premium, scarce.

### DO NOT ADD (these read underground / edgy / cheap)
- ❌ Bottle-service / VIP-table clichés, sparklers, cash-fanning, money imagery.
- ❌ Generic smiling stock models or "diverse team high-fiving" stock.
- ❌ EDM-festival pyro / laser-wall stock and **heavy purple-green club lighting** (instantly reads cheap).
- ❌ Grainy amateur phone snaps, flash-photo party pics, anything that looks like a promoter's camera roll.
- ❌ Real venue/artist **logos, recognizable faces, or ticketed-brand marks** (legal + cheapens it).
- ❌ Literal screenshots of text-message threads, emojis-as-graphics, meme energy.
- ❌ Sweaty, chaotic, or intoxicated-looking crowds. Suggest the night; don't expose it.
- ❌ More than one photographic grade. Mixing warm + cool + b/w photos is the fastest way to look amateur.

## PART 6 — Exclusivity, demand & access *without* looking underground

1. **Silhouette + negative space.** Show the *shape* of the crowd, not its face. Suggestion = class; documentation = flyer.
2. **One disciplined grade.** Pick a single look and force every photo into it (see Part 7). This alone moves the deck from "edgy" to "editorial."
3. **Darken everything; let black breathe.** SNATCH IT's black canvas is its luxury. Images should emerge from black, not fill the frame edge-to-edge with brightness.
4. **Red as a scalpel, never a wash.** Keep #ff0033 as a single accent (a light, a sign, type). Red-soaked photos look like a club promo.
5. **Threshold imagery = access psychology.** Ropes, lines, wristbands, a door with light behind — the visual grammar of "granted, not bought" (straight from PLACES).
6. **Golden/blue hour over midnight-strobe.** Cinematic dusk reads aspirational and expensive; strobe-lit chaos reads cheap.
7. **Rhythm.** Alternate atmospheric "feel" slides with clean "fact" slides so demand *builds* instead of flattening (Storylines' mistake).
8. **Fewer, better, bigger.** 6–8 perfect full-bleed frames beat 14 mediocre ones. Restraint signals confidence.

---

## PART 7 — The SNATCH IT grade (the single most important decision)

Choose **one** and apply to every photo:

- **Recommended — "Cinematic Low-Key Film":** deep crushed blacks, desaturated midtones, a controlled warm/amber highlight, subtle film grain, and a *single* red accent allowed to glow. Feels like A24 nightlife / a fashion film. Sits perfectly on the existing black + red system.
- Alternative — **High-contrast monochrome** with one red element (very editorial, very safe, very premium).

Avoid full-color saturated photography — it will fight the brand and read generic.

## TL;DR for execution
1. Pick **one grade** (Cinematic Low-Key Film). 
2. Add full-bleed, darkened atmosphere to **slides 1, 2, 3, 6, 11, 16, 17** (+ subtle on 7, optional 10/13).
3. Keep **8, 9, 12, 15** (and data on 7) **clean black** — that contrast is the premium.
4. Source only: crowds-in-silhouette, phone-glow, Miami dusk, thresholds/access, motion. 
5. Never: bottle-service, stock smiles, pyro/lasers, purple-club lighting, real logos, party snapshots.
6. Goal: by the time an investor reaches the business model, they've already *felt* the demand.
