# SNATCH IT — VC REVIEW MEMO
### Seed round · $3M · Review date: Apr 2026
### Reviewer: Generalist consumer / marketplaces partner

---

## 1. INVESTMENT DECISION

**Verdict: No. Not yet. Pass on this version. Revisit in 90 days if they come back with the gaps closed.**

**Immediate reaction (first 90 seconds):**
The deck *looks* great. The category framing ("marketplace for nightlife access") is real. I'd take the meeting — but I'd walk out of it without a term sheet.

Why I'd pass right now:
- The business looks like a **lifestyle company pretending to be a venture company.** Miami nightlife is a ~$7B regional market. Even at 15% take on 10% of it, that's $100M revenue at ceiling — fine for a PE exit, too small for a $1B+ venture outcome without a credible path beyond nightlife.
- Marketplaces in this space have **a graveyard.** Tablelist, Hush, Discotech, Reserve, Liist, Bottle Menu, Nocturnal — all either pivoted, died, or got acqui-hired for modest outcomes. Nothing in this deck explains why this time is different beyond "Miami" and "auctions."
- The **supply-side math isn't defended.** Top clubs already sell out. Why do they trust a new platform to sell their last 10% of inventory instead of keeping it for their own whales and promoters? The deck assumes venue adoption; it doesn't earn it.
- **Traction is pre-launch vanity** (waitlist + signed venues). "Signed" = LOI or handshake, not paying customers. At seed, I accept this, but I need a much sharper story about the 6 top-10 clubs — are they *contracted* with revenue share, or just "interested"?

This is a 6 on a first read. It could be a 9 with 3–4 slides added and the story tightened.

## 2. WHAT WORKS

**Strategic positives — real signal here:**

- **Category framing is correct.** "Marketplace for nightlife access" is a better positioning than "app for nightclub tables." It gives the business room to expand.
- **Miami as the wedge is the right call.** Highest density of paying customers per square mile in the US for this category. Also: cultural export city — what works in Miami gets copied in Vegas, Dubai, Tulum. Beachhead logic is sound.
- **Auction mechanic is novel in this specific vertical.** Dynamic pricing is inevitable in nightlife; no one has cracked it with a consumer-facing mechanism. This is the real wedge.
- **Take-rate model is clean.** 15% seller-side + 5% buyer-side is in range for marketplaces. Dual-sided monetization is a legit fundamentals signal.
- **Zero-inventory, capital-light** is the right business model for this stage. No float, no legal liability on held tickets.
- **Cultural brand instinct is correct.** "I snatched it" as the flex is exactly the right kind of moat for this category — StockX did this with sneaker drops. If earned, this is the durable moat.
- **Gen Z / UX gap is real.** Promoters still run WhatsApp groups and paper lists. That is a genuine behavioral tailwind and a durable acquisition advantage.

If I were making a small angel check on the founder's conviction and pattern-match to StockX / StubHub, I could see it. At $3M institutional, I need more.

## 3. CRITICAL GAPS

These are ranked by what would block a partner meeting, not by slide order.

### 3.1 Supply-side economics are a black box
The deck tells me 37 venues are "signed." Meaningless without:
- Are these **revenue-share contracts** or soft LOIs?
- What's the **take rate they agreed to** — is 15% realistic in a venue's P&L?
- What's the **inventory commitment** — 5% of tables? 10%? Last-minute only?
- Can venues **cancel at any time**? What happens when a rival platform shows up with 10%?

Without this, the moat slide's "venues can't leave" claim is unfounded.

### 3.2 Demand-side economics are missing
Where is:
- **Average transaction size** (est. $200? $1,200? $5,000? Drives entire model)
- **Repeat rate** — is this a once-a-month user or once-a-year tourist?
- **CAC assumption** — "the brand is the acquisition" is a wish, not a plan
- **LTV / payback period**
- **What % of bidders win vs. leave disappointed** — this is the brutal unit-economics question for auction models

### 3.3 TAM math is not real TAM
$85B "global nightlife + premium experiences" is a fabricated number — it combines unrelated categories.
- What's nightlife *alone*? (Much smaller — probably $30–40B globally, $15B in US)
- What's the *addressable slice* — i.e., tables, VIP, last-minute access? That's likely under $5B globally
- This needs bottom-up math, not top-down category sweep

### 3.4 The Team slide is missing entirely
**This is disqualifying.** At seed, the team slide is 80% of the decision. Not including it means either:
- The team isn't credible (fatal), or
- The founder doesn't understand that at seed, investors bet on people first

Either interpretation is a red flag. Fix immediately.

### 3.5 Competition is positioned lazily
Listing Ticketmaster / Resy / StubHub misses the actual competitive set:
- **Promoter networks** (WhatsApp groups, Instagram DMs) — the dominant current channel, and free
- **Concierge services** — Quintessentially, John Paul, Amex Centurion concierge
- **Venue-direct apps** — Dorsia, Amex Resy, OpenTable's VIP tier
- **Platform incumbents** — Google has been experimenting with event bidding; Airbnb Experiences quietly moved toward premium access
- **Secondary marketplaces pivoting** — StubHub owns the ticket side already and has a $4B balance sheet

"This is a new category" is the right instinct but the deck doesn't earn it.

### 3.6 Moat claims are premature
"Liquidity is the moat" works *after* you have liquidity. At seed, you don't. The actual moat at this stage is:
- Founder relationships with venue owners (show it — name them)
- A 6-month head start on the product (show shipping velocity)
- Brand capture with a specific tastemaker segment (show influencer/host adoption)

### 3.7 Risks slide doesn't exist
At a $3M check, I want to see: "Here are the 3 things that could kill this company, and here's how we mitigate each." Its absence suggests the founder hasn't stress-tested the model. Examples of real risks:
- **Venue churn:** biggest clubs pull out → kills supply
- **Fraud / no-shows:** winner doesn't show, venue loses the seat
- **Regulatory:** Florida liquor license implications, ticket resale law
- **Chargebacks:** 1am drunk bids = morning regret = massive dispute rate
- **Winner-take-most:** in marketplaces, #2 dies. Can you be #1?

### 3.8 Why Now is thin
Three cited statistics, none sourced. "Miami did $7.2B in 2025" — where's the citation? VCs fact-check this stuff in 30 seconds.

### 3.9 Go-to-Market is aspiration, not plan
"Win Miami, then everywhere" is a slogan. What's the actual GTM?
- Which 5 venues close first and why?
- Who are the 50 tastemaker customers who seed demand?
- What's the content/organic loop?
- What's the launch event / moment?

### 3.10 Financials / use of funds missing
The ask slide gives milestones but no:
- **Burn rate** (monthly / quarterly)
- **Use of funds breakdown** (eng % / ops % / marketing %)
- **Runway math showing $3M = 24 months**
- **Headcount plan**
- **Series A readiness criteria** — what metrics make the next round raisable at a doubled valuation?

## 4. WHAT NEEDS TO BE ADDED (ACTIONABLE)

### Slide to ADD: "Why Us" / Founder-market fit
**What it includes:** Founder name + 1-line credential tied directly to *why they can win Miami nightlife.* Ideal: "Operated 3 Miami venues. 5,000+ nights on door." Or: "Built StubHub's first mobile app. Shipped $800M GMV." Or: "Raised $8M for a prior consumer marketplace, exited to [X]."
**Why it matters:** Seed checks are bets on the founder's right to win *this specific problem.* No team slide = no trust.

### Slide to ADD: Team
**What it includes:** Founder + 1–2 co-founders. Environmental photos. One line each on why each person is an unfair advantage for this exact problem. Bench of 3–5 advisors with direct nightlife/marketplace operating experience (not "famous VCs who met with us once").
**Why it matters:** Standard seed-deck slide. Its absence alone will cost you meetings.

### Slide to ADD: Real Unit Economics
**What it includes:**
- Avg transaction size: $X (from beta)
- Take rate: 15% + 5% = $Y per transaction
- Buyer CAC at launch / steady state
- Repeat rate / monthly cohort behavior
- Contribution margin per transaction
- Payback period in months
**Why it matters:** Marketplace investors underwrite economics, not vision. No UE math = no check.

### Slide to ADD: Supply Deal Terms
**What it includes:** Representative venue deal — length of exclusivity, inventory commitment, take-rate negotiation, termination clauses. One named venue as a case study: *"E11EVEN commits 15 tables/weekend, 3-year minimum, 12% take, exclusive in the 'last-minute' category."*
**Why it matters:** Proves venue side is contracted, not aspirational. Directly kills the #1 objection.

### Slide to ADD: Risks & Mitigations
**What it includes:** Top 3 company-killer risks. One line of mitigation per risk.
**Why it matters:** Shows founder maturity. Investors write checks to founders who've *already thought about what could kill them.*

### Slide to ADD or FIX: Financials / Use of Funds
**What it includes:** 24-month P&L summary. Headcount plan. % allocation of the $3M (e.g., 60% eng, 20% ops, 15% marketing, 5% legal). Monthly burn at full hire. Series A milestones.
**Why it matters:** Anything less = "founder doesn't know how to spend money responsibly."

### Slide to FIX: Competition
**What it includes:** Real competitive landscape. Include: promoter WhatsApp networks, Dorsia, Resy, Amex concierge, Tock VIP, StubHub experimental. Honest 2x2 with real axes (e.g., *last-minute ↔ advance booking*, *auction ↔ fixed*). Structural defensibility answer.
**Why it matters:** "No competition" or a lazy competitor list signals a founder who hasn't done the work.

### Slide to FIX: Traction
**What it includes:** Replace LOIs with contracted partners. Replace waitlist count with actual pre-sold GMV. Replace press quote with a specific repeat behavior metric from beta (e.g., "34% of beta bidders bid on a second night within 14 days").
**Why it matters:** Investors discount LOI-traction heavily. Actual paid transactions — even small — convert to conviction 10x faster.

### Slide to FIX: Market Size
**What it includes:** Bottom-up TAM. E.g., *"120K premium nightlife transactions/weekend in Miami × avg $1,200 transaction × 52 weekends = $7.5B city-level GMV opportunity. At 15% take, $1.1B SAM per major market. We own 5 markets = $5.5B SAM."* Cited sources.
**Why it matters:** The current TAM is a PowerPoint number. Real marketplaces earn credibility through defensible bottom-up math.

## 5. STRATEGIC REPOSITIONING

### The wrong framing (what's in the deck today)
*"A Miami nightlife auction app."*
That's a feature. Not a venture outcome.

### The right framing
*"The liquidity layer for scarce access."*

This is a company that:
- Starts with nightlife because nightlife has the highest frequency + highest urgency + lowest switching cost.
- Expands into every category where "price < demand" creates a broken secondary market: dining, travel, sports, fashion, wellness, private aviation.
- Becomes the default infrastructure for dynamic pricing in experiences — the way Stripe became infrastructure for payments.

### Category to own
**"The access economy."** Not nightlife. Nightlife is the wedge.

The pitch becomes: *"Every scarce experience in the world is priced wrong. We fix it — starting with the loudest, highest-frequency market: Miami nightlife."*

### How to make this a "must invest"
1. **Prove the wedge is ownable.** Lock 20+ top Miami venues on exclusive terms. That alone is defensible.
2. **Show first dollar of GMV.** Even $50K of real transactions crushes 4,200 waitlist signups.
3. **Name the cultural moment.** Point to a specific shift — e.g., "Dorsia raised $24M in 2024 on the same thesis; we're 18 months ahead of them in Miami."
4. **Show the expansion roadmap in one line.** "Nightlife → Restaurants → Travel → Sports." Don't put it in a slide; drop it in a sentence.
5. **Raise on cultural gravity, not technology.** This deck should feel like a preview of a brand that *will* define the next decade of experience commerce.

## 6. TRACTION ANALYSIS

### Is it convincing?
**No. It's suggestive, not convincing.**

What's shown:
- 4,200 waitlist (100% organic) — decent signal, but waitlists are the lowest-fidelity metric in consumer
- 37 venues "signed" — could mean anything from "we have a contract" to "they like us at happy hour"
- $140K in pre-auction bids — interesting but unverifiable
- Press quote from *Ocean Drive* — good cultural signal, not investable signal

### What would make it stronger
- **Actual GMV through the platform.** Even $50K of completed transactions is stronger than $140K pledged.
- **Cohort retention.** If the 200 beta users have a 40% M2 retention, that's a defensible consumer marketplace.
- **Named, contracted venues.** "LIV, E11EVEN, and Space Miami have each committed 10 tables/weekend on exclusive auction terms through 2027."
- **One repeat auction story.** "We ran 12 auctions for LIV VIP tables in March. Avg bid: $1,340. Venue's average table sale: $920. Net lift: +$420/table."
- **Acquisition velocity.** "Waitlist grew 40% WoW for 8 weeks straight — zero paid."
- **Organic referral coefficient (K-factor).** If K > 0.5, this is a viral product and the deck should scream it.

### Missing metrics to pull before re-pitching
1. Avg auction settlement price vs. venue's list price (lift per transaction)
2. Win rate (bids placed → auctions won)
3. Repeat bidder rate within 30 days
4. Venue net revenue lift (proves the supply-side pitch)
5. Weekend concentration of demand (Thursday/Friday/Saturday split)
6. Monthly GMV trajectory, not just cumulative
7. Organic vs. paid acquisition split
8. NPS / influencer endorsement count

## 7. FINAL VERDICT

### Score: 6 / 10

Polished visuals. Strong instinct on category. Right wedge. Right city. Wrong level of business discipline for a $3M check.

### What would make it a 9
- Add Team slide with founder-market fit that's obvious
- Replace traction with real GMV + cohort retention (even at small scale)
- Contractualize the supply side — 10 named exclusive venues with deal terms in appendix
- Show bottom-up TAM with cited sources
- Add Risks + Mitigations slide
- Complete Financials (burn, use of funds, Series A readiness criteria)
- Real competitive landscape (promoters, Dorsia, concierge, StubHub)
- One repeat auction case study with the lift math

### What would make it a 10
Everything above + one of the following:
- **Signature fact.** "In our first 60 days, we've processed $420K GMV at an avg 34% lift per transaction. 41% of bidders bid again within two weeks."
- **Signature contract.** "E11EVEN has granted us exclusive last-minute inventory rights through 2028."
- **Signature founder.** "[Name] founded and operated 3 of Miami's top-20 nightclubs over the last decade."
- **Signature endorsement.** "Lead investor: [a household-name consumer VC with pattern-match to StockX or Airbnb]."

Any one of those four turns this from a "take the meeting" deck into a "fight for allocation" deck.

---

## THE BOTTOM LINE

**This is a real company. It is not yet a fundable company.**

The instincts are right. The category is real. The wedge is correct. The design signals founder taste — which matters in this vertical specifically.

What's missing is the unglamorous work: supply-side contracts, unit economics, team credibility, and actual paid transactions.

Close those gaps and this raises $3M comfortably at an $18–22M post-money valuation. Leave them open and this raises a $500K friends & family round — or nothing at all.

**I'd take a follow-up call in 90 days.**
